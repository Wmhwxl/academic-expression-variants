# Field And Section Style Notes

Read this only when field, venue family, or manuscript section affects style.

## General Scientific English

- Prefer concrete verbs over nominal stacks.
- Keep one main claim per sentence when the source is complex.
- Avoid decorative phrasing, hype, and excessive signposting.
- Preserve methodological and evidential boundaries.

## Computer Science, AI, CV, NLP, Systems

- Favor precise task, model, data, metric, and baseline wording.
- "Outperform" requires a benchmark or comparison in the source.
- "Generalize" requires explicit cross-domain, cross-dataset, or held-out evidence.
- For method text, specify what is optimized, predicted, aligned, encoded, decoded, retrieved, or regularized.
- For ablations, use neutral verbs: "removing X degrades Y", "X contributes to Y", "the effect is most pronounced under Z".

## Biomedical, Clinical, Public Health

- Avoid clinical efficacy claims unless trials or clinical endpoints are provided.
- Distinguish association, prediction, diagnosis, treatment, mechanism, and causation.
- Preserve population, model organism, tissue, cell line, and endpoint boundaries.
- Use cautious implication language: "may inform", "is associated with", "suggests a potential role".

## Economics, Management, Social Science

- Separate identification, correlation, mechanism, and policy implications.
- Avoid causal wording unless the design supports it.
- Be explicit about setting, sample, treatment, outcome, and identification strategy.
- For theory claims, distinguish "consistent with", "supports", and "establishes".

## Engineering, Materials, Energy

- Keep units, operating conditions, device configuration, and performance metrics unchanged.
- Avoid "stable", "scalable", or "industrial" unless the source gives evidence.
- Use precise comparative framing: "relative to", "under", "at", "after", "across".

## Section Guidance

### Abstract

- Use dense but readable sentences.
- Preserve result magnitude and conditions exactly.
- Avoid adding background not present in the source.

### Introduction

- Strengthen problem-gap-contribution flow.
- Do not overstate novelty or field-wide importance.

### Methods

- Prefer reproducible, procedural clarity.
- Avoid rhetorical flourish.

### Results

- State what was observed, measured, or estimated.
- Separate results from interpretation.

### Discussion

- Balance interpretation with limitations and scope.
- Use implication language proportionate to evidence.

### Rebuttal

- Be respectful, direct, and evidence-centered.
- Avoid "obviously", "clearly", "misunderstood", or blame-framing.
